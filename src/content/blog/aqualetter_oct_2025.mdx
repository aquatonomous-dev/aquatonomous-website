Welcome to the **aQuatonomous Newsletter (Fall 2025 | October Edition)**.

After wrapping up an incredible 2024–25 season, we’re back for another year of innovation, collaboration, and impact. We represented Queen’s University at the international RoboBoat competition in Florida and earned recognition as one of the top design teams on campus. Now we’re taking what we learned and pushing harder.

## Our Mission Continues

Founded by women at Smith Engineering, aQuatonomous is dedicated to advancing autonomous marine technology for the good of the planet. We design, build, and program **Autonomous Surface Vehicles (ASVs)**, self-navigating boats that support ecological research and sustainability.

Every project we take on is guided by our four mission pillars:

- Innovation in Autonomous Marine Tech
- Robotics for the Good of the Planet
- Fieldwork and Professional Development
- Robotics for Everyone

We’re proud that every initiative aligns with at least one **United Nations Sustainable Development Goal (UN SDG)**, especially those related to climate action, clean energy, and life below water.

## Team Growth and Recruitment

After a highly successful recruitment season, aQuatonomous has welcomed a talented new cohort of members across all sub-teams. With diverse backgrounds in engineering, computing, environmental science, and design, new recruits are already contributing ideas and energy to projects spanning mechanical innovation, autonomy, and ecological research.

This growing team marks one of the largest and most multidisciplinary lineups in our club’s history.

## What’s New This Year

With hiring season officially wrapped up, our dynamic 2025–26 crew is ready to dive in. We’ve expanded across five sub-teams:

- **Mechanical** is refining hull design for stability and performance.
- **Electrical** is building a smarter, safer power system and exploring solar charging.
- **Autonomy** is pushing our AI navigation systems to the next level using computer vision and path-planning algorithms.
- **Systems Integration** is connecting hardware and software through simulation and telemetry.
- **EcoSci** is applying our ASV technology to real environmental data collection, working with labs and local communities.

Our Business Team has also hit the ground running by developing sponsorship packages, managing outreach, and crafting this newsletter to keep partners connected and informed.

![What’s New This Year page](/photos/aqualetter_oct_2025/whats-new.png)

### Sub-team highlights

#### Mechanical: Hull Printing Progress
The Mechanical Team has begun 3D-printing hull sections using newly arrived filament, marking a major step in this year’s boat design. Early test prints have shown excellent structural performance and precision, setting the stage for full-scale fabrication of our 2025–26 hull.

#### Electrical: Heat Dissipation and Solar Development
Focused on enhancing power efficiency and safety, the Electrical Team is exploring new heat dissipation solutions for high-power components and developing integrated solar charging systems to improve sustainability and system endurance.

#### Systems Integration: Bridging Software and Hardware
The Systems Integration Team is ensuring seamless communication between software and hardware through MAVROS integration, connecting the Jetson, Pixhawk, and motor controllers to achieve precise, real-time navigation.

## Looking Ahead: Goals for 2025–26

This year’s theme is **Integration and Impact**. After a successful competition debut, our focus now shifts toward refinement, research, and community engagement.

Our top priorities include:

- Upgrading the ASV with improved stability, data systems, and autonomy performance.
- Publishing our first technical and sustainability reports to share findings from the 2025 RoboBoat season.
- Expanding community involvement, from participating in environmental conferences to building partnerships with local organizations in Kingston.
- Launching new media channels. We’re setting sail on TikTok and YouTube to showcase our progress, projects, and behind-the-scenes fun.

We’re also exploring how aQuatonomous can contribute to open-source robotics communities by sharing knowledge that empowers others to design sustainable technologies.
